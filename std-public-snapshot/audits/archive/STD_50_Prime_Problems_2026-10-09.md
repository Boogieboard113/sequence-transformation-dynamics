# STD and the fifty prime number problems

Date: October 9, 2026

All fifty problems receive a mathematical treatment below. The principal result is an exact, finite-dimensional transfer law for every fixed-width occupancy pattern across complete primorial periods. It supplies gap and constellation counts without constructing the full period. It does not supply the missing estimates for actual primes in intervals.

The proofs here are self-contained mathematical derivations, supported by executable exact checks. They have not received independent review. The CRT identities, sieve methods, gap-driving recurrences, and singular-series limits have substantial prior literature. No new theorem about the distribution of primes, or claim of mathematical priority, is established.

## 1. Corrections and scope

The consecutive prime gaps starting at 2 are

\[
1,2,2,4,2,4,2,4,6,2,6,4,2,\ldots.
\]

The initial 2 in the proposed list was an error.

Two deletion operations must be distinguished:

* The candidate filter \(T_p\) deletes **every** integer divisible by \(p\), including \(p\).
* The ordinary Eratosthenes operation \(E_p\) deletes multiples \(n>p\), preserving the prime \(p\) itself.

Thus repeated \(T_p\) does not leave the prime sequence on the positive integers. On a fixed finite interval, sufficiently many such filters leave only 1. The periodic candidate model is nevertheless exactly the appropriate model for wheels and primorial gaps.

For squarefree \(M\), define

\[
s_M(n)=\mathbf1_{\gcd(n,M)=1},\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
\]

periodically modulo \(M\). Set \(s_1(n)=1\). Let \(G(M)\) be the cyclic gaps between the reduced residues modulo \(M\). All cyclic counts include wraparound. Ordinary intervals do not wrap around.

The existing STD audits define \(C_h\) on **all finite cyclic words over a fixed finite alphabet**, and treat synchronous local emission tables. Arithmetic masks have external residue phases; prime gaps have unbounded values; the wheel source family is highly restricted. Consequently, the audit's full-source rank test and global-closure theorem cannot simply be imported as arithmetic theorems. The arguments below explicitly introduce the relevant arithmetic state.

“Finite-dimensional” means finitely many counters for a fixed requested observable. Those counters can have growing bit lengths. It does not mean finitely many possible states, constant memory, or a polynomial-time solution when the requested window width also grows.

## 2. Exact transfer for every fixed window

Fix a width \(W\ge1\), with positions \(I=\{0,\ldots,W-1\}\). For \(A\subseteq I\), let

\[
B_M(A)=\#\{n\bmod M:\{i\in I:s_M(n+i)=1\}=A\}.
\]

This is the ordinary binary \(W\)-block histogram in subset notation. It has at most \(2^W\) coordinates and sums to \(M\).

### Theorem A. Complete-period transfer without retained phase

Let \(q\) be a prime not dividing \(M\). Define a matrix indexed by subsets of \(I\):

\[
K_q(B,A)=\#\{r\bmod q:
B=A\setminus\{i\in I:i\equiv r\pmod q\}\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{B_{qM}=K_qB_M.}
\]

Every entry is a nonnegative integer and every column sums to \(q\). The matrix depends on the window and on \(q\), not on \(M\).

**Proof.** Each starting residue \(n\bmod M\) has the \(q\) lifts \(n+tM\bmod qM\). Its old occupancy pattern is identical at every lift. Since \(M\) is invertible modulo \(q\), the residues \(-n-tM\bmod q\) run through all classes once. At the lift with this class equal to \(r\), exactly the old occupied offsets congruent to \(r\) are erased. Summing the resulting patterns proves the formula. This proof allows \(W>M\): old windows then use periodic wrapping. QED.

The proof works more generally for **any binary periodic input of period \(M\)**, lifted and masked by \(q\), provided \(\gcd(M,q)=1\). It does not require that the old pattern was already generated by a sieve.

The theorem answers the central phase question in one important setting: **full-period fixed-window statistics need no additional phase counter.** All new phases are averaged exactly by the lifts. An arbitrary interval does not enjoy this property.

For \(q>W-1\), distinct offsets lie in distinct classes modulo \(q\). The update becomes particularly simple. A pattern with \(r\) occupied sites has \(q-r\) unchanged copies and one copy with each individual occupied site removed.

### Theorem B. Correlation coordinates diagonalize every transfer

For \(H\subseteq I\), define the nonexclusive pattern count

\[
N_M(H)=\sum_{n\bmod M}\prod_{h\in H}s_M(n+h),
\qquad N_M(\varnothing)=M.
\]

Set \(\nu_q(H)=|H\bmod q|\), including \(\nu_q(\varnothing)=0\). Then

\[
\boxed{N_{qM}(H)=(q-\nu_q(H))N_M(H)},
\qquad
\boxed{N_M(H)=\prod_{p\mid M}(p-\nu_p(H)).}
\]

Indeed, of the \(q\) lifts of an old occurrence, exactly \(\nu_q(H)\) have a forbidden new phase. Starting at \(M=1\) proves the product formula.

The coordinate conversion is invertible:

\[
N_M(H)=\sum_{A\supseteq H}B_M(A),
\qquad
B_M(A)=\sum_{J\subseteq I\setminus A}(-1)^{|J|}N_M(A\cup J).
\]

These are subset summation and inclusion-exclusion. If \(Z\) is the subset-summation matrix, then

\[
ZK_qZ^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}_{H\subseteq I}(q-\nu_q(H)).
\]

In particular, all these matrices commute. This commutation does not authorize repeatedly treating the same prime as new: the physical lifting theorem requires \(q\nmid M\).

**Cost.** A sparse update uses \(O(W2^W)\) integer operations per prime, with counters of \(O(\log M)\) bits. CRT formulas also compute individual correlations directly. This avoids enumerating \(M\) locations, but is exponential in \(W\), and the prime list itself must be supplied or generated.

## 3. Gap patterns and their compressed evolution

Let positive gaps \(d_1,\ldots,d_k\) have total span \(D\). Their required occupied offsets are

\[
H=\{0,d_1,d_1+d_2,\ldots,D\},
\qquad J=\{0,\ldots,D\}\setminus H.
\]

The exact count of this **consecutive** cyclic gap pattern is

\[
\boxed{C_{(d_1,\ldots,d_k)}(G(M))
=\sum_{L\subseteq J}(-1)^{|L|}N_M(H\cup L).}
\]

The expression requires all vertices in \(H\) to survive and all intervening sites to be erased. It distinguishes a constellation of occupied sites from a sequence of consecutive gaps. For patterns whose span can exceed a period, the convention is to follow the periodically extended sequence; the same occupancy identity still applies.

In particular, for a single gap \(g\),

\[
n_{g,1}(M)=\sum_{L\subseteq\{1,\ldots,g-1\}}
(-1)^{|L|}\prod_{p\mid M}(p-\nu_p(\{0,g\}\cup L)).
\]

Theorem A with \(W=D+1\) therefore closes every gap-pattern query of bounded **numerical span**. A bound on the number of gaps alone is not a bound on span.

### Theorem C. The established driving-term recurrence

Assume \(2\mid M\), \(q\nmid M\) is an odd prime, and \(g<2q\). Let \(n_{g,j}(M)\) count runs of \(j\) consecutive cyclic gaps with sum \(g\). Then

\[
\boxed{n_{g,j}(qM)=(q-j-1)n_{g,j}(M)+j\,n_{g,j+1}(M).}
\]

The \(j\)-coordinates needed for even \(g\) are at most \(1\le j\le g/2\). Set coordinates beyond that range to zero.

**Proof.** All surviving vertices are odd, so their pairwise distances are even. Within span \(g<2q\), no two vertices can have the same residue modulo odd \(q\): a nonzero even multiple of \(q\) is at least \(2q\). A length-\(j\) driver has \(j+1\) vertices whose deletions therefore affect distinct lifted copies. Two endpoint deletions destroy the driver. Its \(j-1\) interior deletions shorten it. The other \(q-j-1\) copies are unchanged. Conversely, a driver of length \(j+1\) has \(j\) interior vertices whose deletion produces length \(j\). At most one deletion occurs within any eligible copy, so these cases exhaust the count. QED.

This recurrence and its population interpretation are established work of Holt and Rudd, with later consolidation by Holt [R1–R3]. The present contribution is its placement alongside the general fixed-window transfer, scope checks, and executable comparison.

For the vector \(v_g=(n_{g,1},\ldots,n_{g,J})^T\), the transfer matrix has diagonal \(q-2,q-3,\ldots,q-J-1\) and superdiagonal \(1,2,\ldots,J-1\). Its eigenvalues are explicit. Under normalization by the twin-gap population, they are

\[
1,\ \frac{q-3}{q-2},\ldots,\frac{q-J-1}{q-2}.
\]

This small state suffices for each fixed \(g\) once \(g<2q\). Before that threshold, simultaneous deletions must be retained; Theorem A handles them exactly.

### Theorem D. Consecutiveness becomes typical conditional on a fixed constellation

Let \(H\subseteq\{0,\ldots,D\}\) be a fixed admissible set containing both endpoints. Write \(M_z=\prod_{p\le z}p\) and \(\rho_z=\varphi(M_z)/M_z\). Then

\[
\frac{B_{M_z}(H)}{N_{M_z}(H)}\longrightarrow1.
\]

Here \(B\) uses the full width \(D+1\); it forbids every other occupied site in the span. A quantitative form is \(1-B/N=O_H(\rho_z)\).

**Proof.** For any interior \(t\notin H\), count the occurrences having this additional survivor. If \(H\cup\{t\}\) is inadmissible, its count is eventually zero. Otherwise, for every prime \(p>D\), the ratio of the new and old local factors is

\[
\frac{p-|H|-1}{p-|H|}=1-\frac1{p-|H|}.
\]

Its product tends to zero because the sum of prime reciprocals diverges. Dividing this product by \(\prod(1-1/p)\) gives a convergent positive product, since the logarithmic difference is \(O_H(p^{-2})\). Thus each additional-site probability is \(O_H(\rho_z)\). A union bound over the finitely many interior sites proves the assertion. QED.

Consequently, for each fixed even \(g\), the known wheel-gap limit follows directly:

\[
\boxed{\frac{n_{g,1}(M_z)}{n_{2,1}(M_z)}
\longrightarrow\prod_{\substack{p\mid g\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}.}
\]

Once every prime divisor of \(g\) has entered the sieve, the ratio of the **nonconsecutive** endpoint counts is already exactly this product. Theorem D removes intermediate survivors asymptotically. This is a wheel result, already represented in the literature [R1–R3], not a result about the frequency of actual prime gaps.

## 4. Twin candidates, correlations, and normalization

For \(H=\{0,2\}\),

\[
N_{M_z}(H)=\prod_{2<p\le z}(p-2),\qquad z\ge2.
\]

Let \(b_z(n)=s_{M_z}(n)s_{M_z}(n+2)\), and let expectation mean uniform averaging over one period. For any shift \(d\),

\[
\mathbb E[b_z(n)b_z(n+d)]
=\frac{N_{M_z}(\{0,2,d,d+2\})}{M_z}.
\]

Subtracting \((N_{M_z}(\{0,2\})/M_z)^2\) gives the exact covariance. Repeated offsets are removed from the set. Higher correlations use the union of all shifted pairs; there is no independence assumption.

For example, two twin-candidate starts separated by 2 would require \(n,n+2,n+4\) all to survive. These occupy every class modulo 3, so the joint count is zero once 3 is sieved. The exceptional actual triple \(3,5,7\) is absent from the all-multiples candidate model because that model removes 3 itself.

For a fixed \(k\)-element set \(H\),

\[
\frac{N_{M_z}(H)/M_z}{\rho_z^k}
=\mathfrak S_z(H)
=\prod_{p\le z}\frac{1-\nu_p(H)/p}{(1-1/p)^k}.
\]

If \(H\) is inadmissible, this becomes zero. If it is admissible, every factor is positive and, beyond the span, each factor is \(1+O_H(p^{-2})\). Therefore the product converges to a positive constant. This proves convergence of the finite-sieve singular-series normalization. The connection to the Hardy–Littlewood conjecture is standard [R4–R5].

For twins,

\[
\mathfrak S_z(\{0,2\})
=2\prod_{2<p\le z}\left(1-\frac1{(p-1)^2}\right)
\longrightarrow2C_2.
\]

This limit is a ratio of vanishing densities, not a positive limiting density of twin candidates.

Three different kinds of stabilization must be kept separate:

1. For fixed \(W\), normalized occupancy histograms \(B_{M_z}/M_z\) converge to the all-zero word. A union bound gives probability of any occupied site at most \(W\rho_z\).
2. Singular-series ratios and fixed-gap-to-gap-2 ratios can have nonzero limits.
3. A nondegenerate law for gaps rescaled by their growing mean requires a growing spatial window. The fixed-\(W\) theorem alone does not prove it, especially for actual primes.

## 5. What fails without phase, bounds, or a specified source family

### Theorem E. Arbitrary value deletion can have infinite pair horizon

Use symbols \(A,B\) that survive and \(E\) that is erased. Given any proposed horizon \(h\), choose \(L\ge h\) and the cyclic words

\[
X=AE^LAE^LBE^LBE^L,
\qquad Y=AE^LBE^LAE^LBE^L.
\]

Every length-\(h\) block sees at most one nonerased symbol. Both words have two copies of each retained symbol, identical surrounding erased blocks, and identical length. Hence \(C_h(X)=C_h(Y)\). Their outputs are \(AABB\) and \(ABAB\), with different pair counts. Thus no finite \(h\) suffices for output pairs on this full source family.

This is also a literal divisibility example: take \(A=1,B=3,E=2\) and delete multiples of 2. Output symbol counts do have horizon 1, illustrating that the required horizon depends on the observable.

**External phase is a separate obstruction.** The rooted cyclic binary words 10 and 01 have equal \(C_h\) for every \(h\), since they are rotations. Keeping only coordinate 0 modulo 2 produces different numbers of ones. Therefore even arbitrarily long rotation-invariant histograms cannot recover a fixed external alignment.

These obstructions do not contradict Theorem A: it uses a full coprime lift and counts all phases, rather than applying a single externally aligned mask to a source of fixed length.

For positional deletion of every \(p\)-th site on a phase-labelled source, kept sites are separated by at most one erased site. An output \(k\)-block is determined by a phase-labelled input window of length at most

\[
k+\left\lceil\frac{k-1}{p-1}\right\rceil.
\]

This is a sufficient bound for this specified deletion rule, not a universal minimum for arbitrary sieving.

### Theorem F. Full positional generation needs growing phase

The binary wheel sequence \(s_M\), with squarefree \(M>1\), has least period \(M\).

**Proof.** A least period \(d\) of an \(M\)-periodic bi-infinite sequence divides \(M\). If \(d<M\), choose a prime \(q\mid M\) not dividing \(d\). By CRT choose \(n\equiv0\pmod q\), while arranging \(n+d\not\equiv0\pmod p\) for every \(p\mid M\). The condition at \(q\) is possible because \(q\nmid d\). Then \(s_M(n)=0\) and \(s_M(n+d)=1\), a contradiction. QED.

A deterministic unary state machine that emits the entire periodic wheel on successive increments therefore requires at least \(M\) states on its eventual cycle. This is a lower bound for that specific generation model. It is not a lower bound against arbitrary algorithms or integer counters. Fixed-window statistics have the far smaller state from Theorem A.

## 6. The missing interval information as an exact defect

Fix an actual finite set of starting integers \(I\) and offsets \(H\). Let \(A_M\) count starts in \(I\) whose offsets all survive the primes dividing \(M\). Before adding \(q\), record

\[
R_q(a)=\#\{n\in I:n\equiv a\pmod q,\ n+H\text{ survives }M\}.
\]

The forbidden classes are \(F_q=-H\bmod q\). Define

\[
\epsilon_q=\sum_{a\in F_q}\left(R_q(a)-\frac{A_M}{q}\right).
\]

Then the exact interval recurrence is

\[
\boxed{A_{qM}=\left(1-\frac{\nu_q(H)}q\right)A_M-\epsilon_q.}
\]

This follows just by subtracting the starts in the forbidden classes. The complete-period lift eliminates this defect by averaging phases. A fixed interval generally does not.

For an admissible \(H\), write \(V_j=\prod_{i\le j}(1-\nu_{p_i}(H)/p_i)\). Iteration gives

\[
\boxed{A_{M_j}=|I|V_j-\sum_{i=1}^j\epsilon_{p_i}\frac{V_j}{V_i}.}
\]

This is an exact representation of the discrepancy, not a bound on it. For example,

\[
|A_{M_j}-|I|V_j|\le\sum_{i=1}^j|\epsilon_{p_i}|\frac{V_j}{V_i}
\]

is valid but can be useless without cancellation or sufficiently strong estimates. Defect control is one concrete route from STD bookkeeping into substantive analytic number theory.

To recover actual primes, take \(z=\sqrt X\) and restrict all tested integers to \((z,X]\). A surviving integer there is prime, because a composite has a prime divisor at most \(\sqrt X\). For twins use starting integers \(z<n\le X-2\); small-prime exceptions are then separated exactly.

### The complete-period density has the wrong constant at this cutoff

Mertens' product theorem gives \(\rho_z\sim e^{-\gamma}/\log z\) [R6]. At \(z=\sqrt X\), replacing the interval count by its full-period density predicts

\[
X\rho_{\sqrt X}\sim2e^{-\gamma}\frac X{\log X}
=1.122918967\ldots\frac X{\log X}.
\]

The prime number theorem has leading coefficient 1. The discrepancy already contributes at main-term scale for single primes.

For twins the analogous substitution predicts

\[
X\rho_{\sqrt X}^{\,2}\mathfrak S(\{0,2\})
\sim8e^{-2\gamma}C_2\frac X{\log^2X}.
\]

Compared with the conjectured Hardy–Littlewood coefficient \(2C_2\), this is larger by

\[
4e^{-2\gamma}=1.260947006\ldots.
\]

The twin comparison is conditional on the conjectured actual asymptotic; the single-prime mismatch is unconditional. Therefore “prove all accumulated defects are negligible relative to the naive square-root-cutoff main term” is the wrong target. One must first recover the correct interval main term. Standard sieve theory and its parity obstruction remain relevant [R7]; changing terminology does not remove that obstruction.

For fixed \(M\), interval candidate counts have a simple exact expression:

\[
A_M([1,X];H)=\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_M(H)}\#\{n\in[1,X]:n\equiv r\pmod M\},
\]

where \(\mathcal R_M(H)\) is the CRT-admissible residue set. Consequently,

\[
\left|A_M([1,X];H)-\frac X M N_M(H)\right|\le N_M(H).
\]

This is useful when there are many full periods, but generally not when \(M\) is enormous relative to \(X\).

## 7. Fifty individual outcomes

The labels below specify the scope of each outcome. “Exact” does not mean a conjecture about actual prime distributions has been solved.

### I. Sieves and deletion dynamics

**1. Eratosthenes as deletion — Exact.** Start with the labelled list \((2,3,\ldots,N)\), and compose the filters \(E_p\) for primes \(p\le\sqrt N\). A composite has a prime factor at most its square root and is deleted; a prime is never deleted. This proves the operator representation. Using \(T_p\) instead produces rough-number candidates and requires the correction in Section 1.

**2. Block-count updates — Exact for specified models.** Theorem A gives the complete binary-block update, including arbitrary simultaneous erasures. Section 3 converts it to consecutive gap-block counts. For arbitrary labelled lists, mark each entry by its keep/delete bit and sum the old gaps between successive kept entries. Boundary gaps on an ordinary interval need separate treatment; they must not be silently wrapped.

**3. Minimum sufficient horizon — No universal finite answer.** For the full alphabet containing two kept symbols and one erased symbol, Theorem E proves infinite horizon for output pairs, while horizon 1 suffices for output symbol counts. For fixed-width periodic occupancy queries, Theorem A gives a width-preserving sufficient state after a full coprime lift. It does not prove that all \(2^W\) coordinates are minimal. For a single predetermined wheel at each stage, an information-minimality question is vacuous unless the allowed source family and computation model are specified.

**4. Finite-dimensional prediction under repeated sieving — Exact at fixed width.** Keep the \(2^W\)-counter vector and apply successive \(K_p\), or keep only the correlations required by a specified query and update their scalar factors. This works for arbitrarily many distinct sieve primes. It does not give fixed precision, a uniform bound in window width, or a finite-state generator of the full wheel. Theorem F provides the latter model's obstruction.

**5. Invariants — Exact with qualifications.** Deletion preserves the labels and relative order of survivors, and preserves the distance between any two retained endpoints. Gap merging preserves their total span. In a fixed cyclic universe with at least one survivor, the sum of gaps is the circumference; after lifting from \(M\) to \(qM\), it becomes \(qM\), not \(M\). Thus total gap length is conserved by deletion within the lifted universe, but not unchanged across growing primorials. Reflection of the reduced-residue set yields reversal symmetry of wheel gap counts. General gap frequencies are not invariants.

**6. Inverse sieving — Exact fiber description.** On a specified finite universe \(U\) of distinct ordered labels, let \(D\subseteq U\) be the deleted labels. An output set \(Y\subseteq U\setminus D\) has precisely the predecessors \(Y\cup Z\), \(Z\subseteq D\), hence \(2^{|D|}\) predecessors if arbitrary subsets of \(U\) are allowed as sources. If the source is already prescribed as the complete interval, it is known and there is no inverse uncertainty. With arbitrary repetitions and no length bound, inserting erased labels gives infinitely many predecessors.

**7. Deletion order — Exact for value filters.** Fixed-label divisibility filters commute and are idempotent. The survivor set after any specified collection of primes is independent of order. Intermediate stages differ because they may contain different collections of filters; stage sizes and patterns change accordingly. Evolving-rank deletions, such as lucky-sieve steps, need not commute. Applying rank-2 and rank-3 deletions to \((1,\ldots,8)\) in opposite orders gives \((1,3,7)\) and \((1,4,7)\).

**8. Matrix representation — Exact.** On coordinates indexed by \(2\le n\le N\), use diagonal matrices \(P_p(n,n)=\mathbf1_{p\nmid n\text{ or }n=p}\). The prime-indicator vector is \(\prod_{p\le\sqrt N}P_p\mathbf1\). For candidate filters use \(D_p(n,n)=\mathbf1_{p\nmid n}\). These are commuting projections. Compacting the list uses rectangular selection matrices whose dimensions change; those should not be confused with the fixed-coordinate diagonal matrices.

**9. Stabilization — Several answers, depending on normalization.** Fixed-width wheel histograms converge to all zeros after normalization by period. Fixed constellation singular-series ratios converge positively when admissible. Fixed-gap ratios converge as in Theorem D. A nondegenerate growing-scale distribution is a separate question, not established here. An ordinary finite interval ultimately empties under all-multiple deletion except possibly for 1.

**10. Information loss versus the lucky sieve — Exact conditional model, no universal ranking.** Give \(N\) source locations independent fair-bit payloads and use a known deterministic deletion mask retaining \(r\) locations. Conditional entropy lost is exactly \(N-r\) bits, for either sieve. At equal retention, this payload model makes their loss equal. If the mask depends on payloads, its own information must be included; survivor-count alone no longer determines loss. With the deterministic complete integer source and known rule there is no Shannon input uncertainty. A claim that one sieve is inherently more informative is therefore undefined until the prior and observation are fixed.

### II. Gap transformations

**11. Deleting selected vertices — Exact.** If retained indices are \(i_1<i_2<\cdots\), the new gap from \(i_j\) to \(i_{j+1}\) is \(\sum_{r=i_j}^{i_{j+1}-1}g_r\). Deleting one interior vertex merges its two neighboring gaps. For a cyclic sequence the same formula wraps; for a finite sequence removed endpoints change the total retained span.

**12. Survival of a gap pattern — Exact.** A pre-existing consecutive pattern survives unchanged precisely when all its vertices survive. During a full new-prime lift, the unchanged-copy count is \(q-\nu_q(H)\) for its cumulative-offset set \(H\). This only counts unchanged copies. Other longer patterns can merge into the same target, so the full update also needs their contributions. Section 3 includes those automatically.

**13. Can local statistics predict later gaps — Yes within stated closure, false universally.** Fixed-span complete-period queries close by Theorem A; fixed gap sizes eventually close by Theorem C. Unphased statistics for an externally masked word can fail even at all horizons. Gap-symbol histograms on arbitrary sequences can also hide the long erased runs of Theorem E. These are distinct source families, so neither positive result licenses a universal prediction claim.

**14. Frequencies of gaps 2, 4, 6, and so on — Exact wheel model, actual-prime asymptotics unresolved here.** Use the driving vectors or inclusion-exclusion to compute wheel frequencies. At \(M=30\), gap counts \((n_2,n_4,n_6)=(3,3,2)\); at \(M=210\), they are \((15,15,14)\). Substituting these frequencies for the distribution of consecutive prime gaps requires interval estimates not provided by this model.

**15. Gap conservation laws — Exact and limited.** Additivity of merged gaps preserves endpoint displacement. The number of gaps decreases by the number of deleted vertices on a nonempty cyclic list. Their sum stays fixed at the current circumference. Thus average gap increases when vertices are deleted. Under lift-then-delete, the gap count changes from \(\varphi(M)\) to \((q-1)\varphi(M)\), and the circumference from \(M\) to \(qM\), so the mean gap is multiplied by \(q/(q-1)\).

**16. Consecutive-gap correlations — Exact for every fixed span.** The joint count of successive gaps \((u,v)\) is the occupied pattern at \(\{0,u,u+v\}\) with every other site in that span unoccupied. Apply Section 3 with width \(u+v+1\). Normalize by \(\varphi(M)\) for a uniformly chosen surviving start. Correlations involving unrestricted, unbounded gaps need a growing family of such queries.

**17. Large gaps — A rigorous construction and finite model, no new asymptotic bound.** Choose distinct primes \(r_1,\ldots,r_L\), then solve \(a+i\equiv0\pmod{r_i}\) for \(1\le i\le L\). CRT produces an empty interval of \(L\) consecutive sites in the corresponding wheel, so its maximum cyclic gap is at least \(L+1\). Taking a sufficiently large positive translate makes those integers composite as well. This reproduces unbounded prime-free intervals. Optimizing the gap against its location, or proving new bounds on maximal prime gaps, remains beyond this construction.

**18. Inverting gap merging — Exact nonuniqueness.** A merged gap \(g\) split into exactly \(j\) positive integer gaps has \(\binom{g-1}{j-1}\) possible ordered refinements. If every old gap must be positive even, the count is \(\binom{g/2-1}{j-1}\), when defined. These are composition counts; actual primality or a specific deletion mask imposes further constraints. Recording all old vertices and the deletion mask makes inversion straightforward; a merged gap alone does not.

**19. Finite-state approximation — Exact for bounded observations; extrapolation unproved.** The fixed-width histogram state is exact for wheel blocks. A finite empirical Markov model can approximate a finite dataset of prime-gap bins, but its transition probabilities, binning, training range, and validation range must be supplied. Stationarity and accuracy at new scales do not follow. The included computations validate exact wheel forecasts, not a learned asymptotic prime predictor.

**20. Evolution across primorial sieves — Exact fixed-span solution.** Theorems A–D solve the finite-period update and its fixed-gap ratio limit, with a small driving state after the stated threshold. For each fixed span, phase can be eliminated by the complete lift. The dimension need not grow with sieve stage; the integer sizes do. A single small state for every unbounded gap pattern and for arbitrary intervals is not established.

### III. Twins and constellations

**21. Twin-candidate observable — Exact.** On the binary sieve sequence use \(N_M(\{0,2\})\); equivalently sum length-3 binary block counts whose endpoints are 1. When 2 is included in \(M\), the middle bit is automatically 0, so this is the 101-block count. On the true prime sequence \(\sum_{n\le X}a_na_{n+2}\) is exact but needs indicator data through \(X+2\).

**22. Twin-candidate counts at each stage — Exact.** The product in Section 4 gives the answer, including the local factor 1 at prime 2. The values at \(M=6,30,210,2310,30030\) are \(1,3,15,135,1485\). The corresponding one-stage multipliers for odd new primes are \(q-2\).

**23. Twin-candidate correlation evolution — Exact at fixed shifts.** For starts separated by \(d\), use the union \(\{0,2,d,d+2\}\) and multiply at the next stage by \(q-\nu_q\) of that union. Separate means and joint moments determine covariance. Overlapping twins demonstrate why treating the two events independently would be wrong.

**24. Finite-state constellation model — Exact.** For a fixed offset set, its one correlation counter and the known local residue cardinalities suffice for nonexclusive counts. For all occupancy patterns in a fixed window, use Theorem A. For exact consecutive constellations, include the interior exclusion terms. These models have different state sizes because they answer different questions.

**25. Sufficient local statistics — Explicit sufficient families, no blanket minimum claim.** To evaluate an exact pattern with occupied set \(H\) and empty set \(J\), it is sufficient to retain \(N_M(H\cup L)\) for all \(L\subseteq J\). Each coordinate evolves independently. The full width-\(D+1\) histogram is another sufficient state. Neither claim establishes minimality on a restricted family of wheel histograms. For one known wheel sequence, arbitrary encodings or recomputation must be excluded before a meaningful lower bound can be proposed.

**26. Stable normalized twin candidates — Proved in the full-period sense.** The density divided by \(\rho_z^2\) converges to \(2C_2\). The unnormalized density tends to zero. This answers one precise normalization question; it does not establish a limiting probability law of actual twins or prove infinitely many of them.

**27. STD interpretation of the singular series — Exact, classical arithmetic content.** Each prime supplies a local survival fraction \(1-\nu_p(H)/p\). Dividing by the independent single-site fraction \((1-1/p)^{|H|}\) and multiplying produces the singular series. The convergence argument appears in Section 4. This interpretation is compatible with classical sieve theory and the W-trick, not a new derivation of the prime-tuples conjecture [R4–R5].

**28. Higher twin-candidate correlations — Exact.** For starts at \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\), take \(H=\bigcup_j\{d_j,d_j+2\}\). Their joint moment is \(N_M(H)/M\), and cumulants can then be computed from the usual finite partition formula. Normalization must use the number of distinct required occupied positions, not automatically \(2m\). An inadmissible union has zero count after its obstructing prime is sieved.

**29. Lucky-pair comparison — Finite measurements and structural distinction.** Prime deletion is tied to integer residue classes. Lucky deletion acts on evolving ranks, so the CRT factorization used above does not transfer automatically. The companion program computes exact finite lucky and prime distance-2 pair counts on the same cutoffs. Similar counts are observations, not a theorem equating their singular series, correlations, or asymptotics.

**30. New actual twin-prime bounds — Not obtained.** Candidate counts give immediate finite upper bounds for actual twin starts above the sieve cutoff, plus explicitly handled small exceptions. These are standard sieve bounds and can be very weak. A new asymptotic upper constant or a positive lower bound at arbitrarily large scales requires control of interval arithmetic beyond the complete-period state. No such estimate is proved here.

### IV. Prime indicators and statistical closure

**31. Block complexity — Rigorous bounds and zero entropy.** Let \(p_a(W)\) count the distinct length-\(W\) blocks of the infinite prime-indicator sequence. Then \(p_a(W)\ge W+1\), since the sequence is not eventually periodic: it has infinitely many ones and arbitrarily long zero runs. For a fixed sieve cutoff \(z\), starts after \(z\) avoid all small-prime classes. With \(M=M_z\) and \(\rho=\varphi(M)/M\), each residue phase has at most \(\rho W+M\) eligible sites. Thus

\[
p_a(W)\le z+M\,2^{\rho W+M}.
\]

For fixed \(z\), divide its logarithm by \(W\), let \(W\to\infty\), and then let \(z\to\infty\). This proves \(\lim_{W\to\infty}\log p_a(W)/W=0\). Exact block complexity and realizability of admissible one-patterns are much harder; admissibility alone is not a proved prime-occurrence criterion.

**32. Horizons on prime indicators — Rule-specific upper bounds, not a universal minimum.** For any length-preserving radius-\(r\) block map on binary sources, an output \(k\)-block is determined by an input \((k+2r)\)-block. This supplies a horizon upper bound on every source family, including prime indicators. Erasing maps require additional assumptions, as Theorem E shows. A single fixed infinite prime sequence is not the audit's full cyclic source family, and the audit's necessity criterion does not establish minima there.

**33. Recoverability after filtering — Exact criterion.** A source statistic \(O\) is recoverable from a filter \(T\) exactly when it is constant on each fiber \(T^{-1}(y)\). If retained original positions are known, prime counts on those retained coordinates are recoverable by summation. Counts or correlations involving deleted coordinates need extra information. A filter retaining only entries equal to 1 outputs an all-one string: its length gives the number of retained primes, but their positions and original gaps are lost unless supplied separately.

**34. Preservation of prime-density information — Not automatic.** For a constant-length binary substitution of length \(\ell\ge1\), with \(r_0,r_1\) ones in the images of 0 and 1, an input of length \(N\) with \(P\) ones has \(r_0(N-P)+r_1P\) output ones. Its input density is recoverable from the output density iff \(r_1\ne r_0\). This is a criterion for the density statistic; it does not settle recovery from the whole output word. Constant maps destroy it, while identity preserves it. Knowing only the limiting prime density 0 does not determine the asymptotic count \(\pi(X)\).

**35. Information content — Distinguish three meanings.** For fixed width \(k\), sampling a uniform starting position up to \(X\) produces an all-zero prime block with probability tending to 1, because the exceptional proportion is at most \(k\pi(X+k)/X\). Its Shannon block entropy tends to zero. Section 7.31 proves zero topological entropy as well. Nevertheless the number of distinct blocks grows. Finally, a deterministic sieve computes the length-\(N\) prefix, so its conditional Kolmogorov complexity given \(N\) is \(O(1)\), and its unconditional description length is \(O(\log N)\). This says nothing about the running time needed to produce it. The prime prefix is not an algorithmically random binary string.

**36. Prime correlations at offsets — Exact identities; general asymptotics not proved.** The observable \(\sum_{n\le X}\prod_{h\in H}a_{n+h}\) is exact. If \(H\) occupies every class modulo some prime \(q\), any prime occurrence must have one of its entries equal to \(q\), leaving only finitely many possible starts. For admissible sets, the candidate formula is exact, but the corresponding actual-prime asymptotic remains a conjectural input in general.

**37. Prime-counting functions — Exact operator formulas.** Sum the vector from Problem 8 to obtain \(\pi(N)\). Alternatively, define \(\Phi(X,z)=\sum_{n\le X}s_{M_z}(n)\). For \(X\ge2\) and \(z=\sqrt X\),

\[
\pi(X)=\Phi(X,\sqrt X)+\pi(\sqrt X)-1.
\]

The 1 removes the surviving integer 1, and the added term restores the small primes removed by the all-multiples filter. This is an exact classical sieve identity, not an error estimate.

**38. Modular structure — Exact and visible.** CRT gives every finite-stage residue obstruction and correlation factor. On actual primes, divisibility exclusions hold with the exceptions where a tested entry equals the sieving prime. These modular restrictions do not make the infinite prime-indicator sequence periodic. Fourier transforming a finite wheel gives a finite harmonic description of that periodic wheel, not a hidden global period of the primes.

**39. Prime versus lucky statistical complexity — Partial comparison only.** Both finite prefixes are computable, so low algorithmic description length is not a distinguishing feature. Their rules differ in value versus rank dependence, their finite counts and pairs differ, and empirical entropy depends on sampling and normalization. The supplied finite data do not establish an asymptotic entropy ordering, matching correlation hierarchy, or shared universality class.

**40. Can finite-block closure bound prime-counting errors — No new bound here.** The histogram of actual prime indicators already contains \(\pi(X)\) in its length-1 count; observing that statistic is not a way to compute its error without input data. Complete-period closure gives arithmetic averages that differ from interval averages. Section 6 isolates the missing defects and shows even the naive leading coefficient can be wrong. No bound of RH strength or other improved prime-counting estimate follows.

### V. Operators and deeper number theory

**41. One operator encoding successive sieve stages — Exact after adding the stage.** Let \(p_j\) be the \(j\)-th prime. On states \((j,M,s_M)\), define the lift-and-mask operator sending the state to \((j+1,p_{j+1}M,s_{p_{j+1}M})\). This is an autonomous map on the enlarged state space. For fixed width, replace the full sequence by \(B_M\), and update with \(K_{p_{j+1}}\). Supplying or computing the next prime is part of the procedure; it is not eliminated by the notation.

**42. Finite spectra — Exact for two different operators.** Label-space deletion projections have eigenvalues only 0 and 1. Pattern-count transfers have eigenvalues \(q-\nu_q(H)\) by Theorem B, and are simultaneously diagonalizable in correlation coordinates. Driving-term matrices have the spectrum in Section 3. These spectra belong to different spaces and should not be conflated.

**43. Limiting spectra — Exact at fixed width, not a general growing-space claim.** Normalize \(K_q\) by \(q\). Its eigenvalues are \(1-\nu_q(H)/q\). As the single new prime tends to infinity, this fixed-dimensional matrix tends to the identity. In contrast, the product over successive primes has eigenvalues \(\prod_p(1-\nu_p(H)/p)\), which tend to zero for nonempty \(H\) and remain 1 for the empty set. Thus the product tends to the rank-one map sending all histogram mass to the empty pattern. Growing-width spectral limits require a common space, embeddings, scaling, and convergence notion not specified by the original question.

**44. Arithmetic functions — Exact weighted transformations.** Initialize \(f(n)=1\) on a finite universe. For each prime \(p\), set coordinates divisible by \(p^2\) to zero and multiply coordinates divisible by \(p\) but not \(p^2\) by \(-1\). After all necessary primes are processed, \(f=\mu\). Dirichlet convolution then gives

\[
\Lambda(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\log(n/d),\qquad
\psi(X)=\sum_{n\le X}\Lambda(n).
\]

These are standard arithmetic identities, expressible as weighted STD operations. Producing the arrays does not prove cancellation bounds for their sums.

**45. Prime errors as defects — Exact reduction, estimates still missing.** Section 6 supplies explicit one-step and telescoping defects for interval sieve counts. Combining them with Problem 37 expresses prime-counting discrepancies in this bookkeeping. Alternatively, \(\psi(X)-X\) is the weighted discrepancy of the \(\Lambda\)-array from uniform mass. These identities identify the quantity needing control; they do not bound it or establish spectral cancellation.

**46. Arithmetic progressions — Exact local candidate formula.** Count starting residues \(n\bmod\operatorname{lcm}(m,M)\) with \(n\equiv a\pmod m\) and \(n+H\) coprime to \(M\). If some \(p\mid\gcd(m,M)\) divides an \(a+h\), the count is zero. Otherwise it is

\[
\prod_{\substack{p\mid M\\p\nmid m}}(p-\nu_p(H)).
\]

This follows by CRT, since each prime already dividing \(m\) has its residue fixed. Passing to finite intervals introduces discrepancies again. No improvement to established bounds on actual primes in progressions is obtained.

**47. Substitution dynamics — A precise partial relationship.** A new wheel stage consists of concatenating \(q\) copies of the old gap cycle and merging at selected vertices. The operation can be represented by a finite transducer when the required phase and stage are included. Its modulus and prime parameter change with stage, so it is not automatically a single substitution on a fixed finite alphabet. A finite-state generator driven only by repeated increments is eventually periodic and cannot generate the prime indicator exactly. This last statement is about unary generation, not about every possible substitution system or digit-input automaton.

**48. Operator model for correlations — Exact.** On \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z/M\mathbb Z)\), let \(D_h\) be multiplication by \(s_M(n+h)\). These are commuting orthogonal projections and

\[
N_M(H)=\operatorname{Tr}\left(\prod_{h\in H}D_h\right).
\]

Dividing by \(M\) gives the uniform correlation. Translation conjugates the \(D_h\). On the pattern space, Theorem B provides another exact representation via simultaneous diagonalization. These models are rigorous finite operator theory, without an asserted link to zeta zeros.

**49. A spectrum related to zeta zeros — Not constructed.** None of the explicit spectra above identifies the imaginary parts of the nontrivial zeta zeros. The identity

\[
-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s},\qquad \Re s>1,
\]

connects the weighted arithmetic data to zeta analytically, but is not a spectral realization or a proof of RH. Such a proposal would need a specified operator and domain, an analytic continuation or trace identity, and a proved correspondence with zeros. No such bridge is established. RH remains listed by the Clay Mathematics Institute as a Millennium problem [R8].

**50. A genuinely new theorem about primes — Not established.** The fixed-window theorem, its implementation, and the phase/interval separation provide a rigorous STD treatment, but the underlying CRT and subset-transform ingredients are classical. The fixed-gap recurrence and ratio limit already have explicit prior art. The work supplies no new actual-prime distribution bound, and no novelty claim should be made without a deeper literature comparison and independent proof review.

## 9. Where STD stands after this work

The finite-period branch now has a concrete mathematical answer: a fixed spatial window admits exact closure under complete coprime lifts, with a positive integer transfer matrix and a diagonal correlation representation. Gap merging, constellation counts, higher candidate correlations, spectra, and several normalized limits can all be treated within that state.

The full-source STD audit remains a separate theory with separate hypotheses. The present results neither invalidate it nor extend it automatically to arbitrary arithmetic source families.

The next substantive research target is the **interval defect**. A useful advance would be an efficiently computed, provably controlled approximation to the residue counts \(R_q(a)\) for a specified range of interval lengths and sieve depths, together with an error bound that improves on direct residue enumeration. The initial benchmark should be single-prime counting, including its correct leading constant, before attempting twin lower bounds. A second, computational target is observable-specific compression of the exact fixed-window state, with a precisely stated family and a lower bound in a chosen counter model.

These targets are concrete. Neither a new twin-prime theorem nor a zeta-spectrum interpretation has been obtained.
