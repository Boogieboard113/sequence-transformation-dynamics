STD AUDIT — CORE v15 AND PERMUTATION EXTENSIONS
10 October 2026, America/Chicago

VERDICT

The reviewed fixed-horizon core and permutation-averaging results survive this
audit. Their main finite computational totals were reproduced. One newer
reset-gap theorem is false under its literal written assumptions because its
proof ignores admissible runs outside the reset orbit. A precise scope repair
and a two-state counterexample are included.

Two global permutation arguments were only sketched in the latest report. The
attached proof supplement supplies explicit padded closed-run witnesses, a
finite periodic-phase test, and constants for the claimed linear lower bound.
These remain internal mathematics, not external review or formal verification.

STD has a credible specialized research core. It is not a completed general
theory of sequence transformations, and its entire implementation is not
formally verified. No meaningful overall percentage-complete follows from
the number of ledger entries.

WHAT THIS AUDIT COVERED

Retrieved the latest canonical theorem ledger v15, the complete Core 0.1
archive, both permutation research reports and their validation summaries,
the reset-gap note and program, support-pair material, and selected earlier
finite-type, cohomological, global-horizon and audit records. Primary external
sources were checked for the rational-relation conjugacy and word-equation
results used below. Input filenames and SHA-256 hashes are in input_provenance.json.

This is a targeted audit of the active prediction-horizon chain, not a reproof
of all 207 historical claims. Optimization, recovery, profile circuits,

covered here. No Lean source was recompiled. No third party reviewed this work.

MATHEMATICAL FINDINGS

1. Fixed-horizon closure: supported in the stated model.

The graph model quantifies over all nonempty closed runs and uses raw cyclic
counts. Additive statistics, connected Euler realizations, component row-space
identities and exact support-pair compatibility fit together coherently.
The open-positive-cone perturbation argument preserves genuine single cyclic
witnesses because positive supports are strongly connected and balanced.
Fixed length/inventory restrictions cannot be added without changing that proof.

Core's bounded minimum-horizon search correctly distinguishes a proved finite
minimum from a finite lower bound. Repeated fixed-order failures alone do not
certify infinity. This distinction remains essential.

2. Reynolds / permutation averaging: supported.

For total permutation actions and a bounded-local additive statistic, finite
ordinary horizon, same-source closed-run invariance, and exact uniform-state
averaging are equivalent. The power-and-average proof works without transitivity.
The synchronized cycle test and bounded-silence estimate also survive review:

    finite H_T(k) implies H_T(k) <= 1+(R+1)(k-1).

For nonerasing machines R=0, this gives H_T(k)<=k whenever finite. For k=1,
every total permutation machine has horizon 1 or infinity, including erasure.
The supplement spells out the missing power-lifting justification needed for
necessity of the exact source-only minimum-rank formula.

Small correction: rational certificates and effective rank algorithms require
rational or otherwise effectively represented exact coefficients. The abstract
characteristic-zero vector-space theorem is more general than its rational
implementation claim. Integer output histogram weights meet the requirement.

3. Global permutation closure and spectrum: supported with proof supplements.

The acyclic-silence reduction to conjugacy of a rational synchronized-loop
relation is sound and relies on an established external decidability theorem.
It is a decision reduction, not a newly implemented polynomial-time STD solver.

Deep Research Pass II's silent-padding and Theta(k) arguments originally omit
their actual rearranged words and quantitative details. The supplement supplies
them using a common-prefix padding lemma and the classical word-square theorem.
This audit therefore does not label the global claims disproved. It supplies
the missing argument instead of treating their validation JSON as proof.

The resulting scoped classification remains: global closure gives either
H_T(k)=1 for all k in the common-periodic case, or H_T(k)=Theta(k) in the
nonperiodic bounded-silence case. Silent cycles under global closure force the
common-periodic branch.

4. Reset-gap theorem: correction required.

The written model restricts b only on the a-orbit of q0 but then speaks about
all closed runs containing b. Consider two states, each with an a self-loop
of weight 0. Put a b self-loop of weight 0 at q0 and weight 1 at the other state.
All the stated orbit assumptions hold. The orbit test reports a finite bound,
but the identical source word b has two different values. Hence H=infinity.

Correct the domain to b-containing closed runs within the a-orbit of q0, or
require every b transition in the entire graph to reset to q0. The residual
theorem and its proof then survive. The supplied program already uses the
stronger implicit reset representation, which explains why its tests pass.

The original command-line script also writes to a hard-coded /mnt/data path;
direct execution failed when that directory was absent. Importing its validate()
function reproduced all results. This is a portability issue, separate from
the mathematical scope error. The audit reproduction wrapper avoids that path.

REPRODUCED EVIDENCE

Core 0.1, normal and optimized Python:

  - 11 named horizon cases passed.
  - 630 periodic marginalization checks passed in each mode.
  - 13 altered horizon certificates were rejected in each mode.
  - All 625 cross-solver decisions agreed: 451 sufficient, 174 insufficient.
  - Six complete reference bundles passed and six instance-binding mutations
    were rejected in each mode.

An initial cross-solver attempt lacked SymPy; installing the pinned 1.14.0
dependency in an isolated task directory resolved it. This was an environment
dependency issue, not a mathematical failure. The primary core tests had already
passed before the dependency was installed.

  - 15,249 direct closed-run comparisons between local emission tables and

  - 180 source padding checks and 170 nonconjugate word-square regressions.

Reset-gap restricted implementation:

  All 500 generated machines reproduce the stated split of 266 finite and 234

  with zero failures. This does not rescue the broader written domain.

Finite tests support the implementations in those scopes. They do not establish
the open bounded-erasure conjecture or universal correctness of Python code.

LEDGER AND REPRODUCIBILITY

The latest canonical ledger contains 207 unique IDs, no missing dependencies,
no dependency cycles, no undefined status labels, and correct status totals.
There are still 60 entries with empty scope fields. Six entries carry inherited
proof-assistant-core status; this audit did not rebuild their proofs and does
not extend that label to the new horizon or permutation code.

The 82 Core archive hashes and five reset-gap archive hashes all match. The
latest permutation deliverables retrieved here are reports and aggregate JSON,
without the original survey programs or per-case certificates. This audit
adds executable independent survey code and data, partially closing that gap.
It does not reconstruct the original runs or verify the reported random sample.

The canonical ledger and original reports remain unchanged. This packet is an
audit and erratum, with supporting derivations, not a silent status promotion.

WHAT REMAINS OPEN

  - A general finite/infinite ordinary-horizon decision procedure for arbitrary
    finite-state transducers, beyond the established structural subclasses.
  - The sharper acyclic-silence permutation conjecture:
        H_T(k)<infinity implies H_T(k)<=k.
    Both exhaustive families support it, but no proof is supplied here.
  - An executable certificate-producing implementation of the full permutation
    global-closure algorithm, including its word-relation decision step.
  - Independent external review and a formally checked certificate kernel.
  - Scalable algorithms and clear contracts connecting the other STD branches.

RECOMMENDED NEXT WORK

First adopt the reset-gap scope erratum and complete the 60 missing historical
scope fields. Package the permutation solver with portable commands, named
models, positive potentials and negative literal runs. Use the explicit proof
supplement as the basis of a reviewed successor ledger. After that consolidation,
the sharp H_T(k)<=k conjecture is the focused mathematical target; arbitrary
transition monoids remain the larger research frontier.
